# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Developers building large, changing directed-graph interfaces are the primary audience. Engineering peers, potential collaborators, and portfolio visitors also need a direct path from working examples to usable documentation and reproducible evidence.

## Product Purpose

Dagr is the GPU renderer for big directed graphs that animate as they change. The maintainer's [2026-10-17 renderer-first decision](specs/roadmap-notes.md#priority-reset-webgpu-renderer-first-2026-10-17-utc) supersedes the 2026-09-29 commerce/VDSL atlas lead direction. Prove WebGPU rendering, animated updates and interaction with named workloads, adapters and measured limits. WebGL2 is the fallback, with parity verified separately.

The System Atlas remains a rich-node VDSL example, not the product identity. Explorer learning and mobile work and the daily report track follow their explicit scheduling in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md). This positioning update does not authorize a showcase rewrite. The all-moving 10k smooth-edge 60 Hz target remains unmet; distinguish measured capability from that goal.

## Brand Personality

Precise, geometric, exploratory. Preserve the existing Dagr mark and the relationship to PRNT's Muslin design system. Dagr is the product identity. The System Atlas demonstrates a consumer-defined visual language.

## Anti-references

Do not lead with the campaign/DND fixture, generic feature-card marketing, fabricated telemetry, or complexity that makes the architecture harder to understand.

## Design Principles

- Show working behavior before explaining implementation detail.
- Let visitors explore relationships and inspect meaning.
- Distinguish source-derived architecture, illustrative examples, and measurements.
- Keep current capabilities and planned editor features separate.
- Preserve experiments as artifacts without making them the public showcase.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA in changed surfaces. Use keyboard-operable controls, visible focus, readable architecture alternatives, reduced-motion support, and labels that do not depend on color or hover alone.
